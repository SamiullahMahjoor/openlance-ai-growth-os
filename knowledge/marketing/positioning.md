# Open Lance Positioning Strategy

This document owns the Positioning strategy for Open Lance: the place it claims in the market. It is a marketing strategy document within the Marketing namespace, and it follows the Marketing Document Standard defined in knowledge/marketing/README.md. Its identity in the inventory is owned by knowledge/marketing/marketing.md; this document owns the strategy only. It consumes company, customer, and competitor knowledge by reference and never restates it, and it applies the brand standards owned by knowledge/brand/ rather than setting them. Where this document and a higher-authority document differ, the higher-authority document governs.

# Purpose

This document owns the strategic decision about how Open Lance is positioned in the market. Its purpose is that every marketing decision expresses one consistent position, so the market understands what Open Lance is and why it exists.

# Strategic Intent

The intent is that both sides of the marketplace, and the wider market, recognize Open Lance as the freelance marketplace built on trust and fairness, and choose it on that basis rather than on price or size alone. This serves the company's mission and vision, owned by knowledge/company/company.md.

# Strategy

- Open Lance is positioned as the trusted, fair freelance marketplace: a marketplace built to protect the people who use it and to treat both sides fairly. The specific differences that set it apart from the alternatives are owned by knowledge/marketing/differentiators.md and are not restated here.
- The position claims trust and fairness, not lowest price and not largest scale. Open Lance does not compete to be the cheapest or the biggest; it competes to be the fairest and most trustworthy.
- The position is held consistently for both sides: businesses are positioned to hire with confidence and protection, and freelancers to work on fair terms and be paid reliably. One position, expressed to two audiences.
- The position is anchored to the company's enduring mission and vision and distinguished against the competitor set, without restating either. What Open Lance is remains owned by knowledge/company/; who it competes with remains owned by knowledge/competitors/.
- The position is durable. It changes only if the company's mission or the market itself changes, through the amendment process.

# Inputs

This strategy consumes the following frozen knowledge by reference and never restates it.

- The company mission, vision, and marketplace model: knowledge/company/company.md.
- The two customer segments it positions to: knowledge/customers/customers.md.
- The competitor set it is distinguished against: knowledge/competitors/competitors.md.
- The differences it draws on: the differentiator strategy at knowledge/marketing/differentiators.md.

# Application

The positioning strategy is the reference every other marketing decision is checked against. Value propositions, the messaging framework, content, channels, and campaigns all express the position, and anything that contradicts it is corrected. This document states the position in principle; it produces no page, tagline, or campaign, which are Outputs.

# Boundaries

This document owns the positioning strategy only. It owns none of the following.

- Company identity, mission, and marketplace model: knowledge/company/company.md.
- Customer segments and their needs: the Customers namespace, knowledge/customers/.
- Competitor identities and records: the Competitors namespace, knowledge/competitors/.
- The promises of value that express the position: knowledge/marketing/value-propositions.md.
- The differences the position draws on: knowledge/marketing/differentiators.md.
- Voice, tone, and the messaging standard used to express the position: the Brand namespace, knowledge/brand/.
- Produced content, taglines, and campaigns: the Output level and the asset systems.

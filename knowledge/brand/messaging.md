# Open Lance Messaging Standard

This document owns the Messaging standard for Open Lance: how the organization expresses what it offers and why it matters. It is a brand standard document within the Brand namespace, and it follows the Brand Document Standard defined in knowledge/brand/README.md. The definition of the term Messaging is owned by knowledge/brand/README.md; this document owns the standard only. The facts a message expresses are owned by knowledge/company/ and knowledge/product/, and the strategy of which message to send, to whom, and where is owned by the Marketing namespace. Where this document and a higher-authority document differ, the higher-authority document governs.

# Purpose

This document owns the standard for how Open Lance builds a message. Its purpose is that every expression of the organization's value is benefit-first, honest, backed by fact, and fair to both sides of the marketplace, whoever or whatever writes it.

# Standard

Open Lance constructs messages by the following durable rules.

- Benefit first, then mechanism. State what the reader gains, then the concrete way Open Lance delivers it. Both the benefit and the mechanism are facts owned by knowledge/company/ and knowledge/product/; a message expresses them and never invents them.
- Every claim is verifiable. Make only claims that a fact owned by knowledge/company/ or knowledge/product/ supports. Never state a value the facts do not support.
- Both sides, fairly. A message serves businesses and freelancers with equal weight, and never sells one side by diminishing the other.
- One idea, said well. Prefer one clear point made strongly to many points made weakly.
- Proof over adjectives. Prefer a concrete fact or outcome to a superlative. Let the fact carry the weight.
- Invite, never pressure. A message invites a decision. It never manufactures urgency, scarcity, or fear to force one.
- Consistent core, adaptive wording. Every message stays true to the enduring purpose owned by knowledge/company/mission.md, while its wording follows the voice and tone standards owned by knowledge/brand/voice.md and knowledge/brand/tone.md.

# Rationale

Messaging is where the organization's mission meets the reader. Because the mission, owned by knowledge/company/mission.md, is a transparent, secure, and fair marketplace, its messaging must earn the same words: benefit-first and proof-backed expression is what transparency sounds like, and equal weight for both sides is what fairness looks like in a message. Claims tied to real facts, rather than to adjectives, keep the promise honest as the product and its facts evolve, because the message points at the fact rather than copying it.

# Application

The messaging standard applies to every outward communication that expresses value, in every medium. When building a message, a writer or agent draws the benefit and the mechanism from the facts owned by knowledge/company/ and knowledge/product/, expresses them by the rules above, and applies the voice and tone standards to the wording. This document sets how a message is built; it does not choose which audiences or channels to pursue, or how the organization is positioned against alternatives, which are owned by the Marketing namespace, and it never writes a specific message, which is an Output.

# Boundaries

This document owns the messaging standard only. It owns none of the following.

- The definition of the term Messaging: knowledge/brand/README.md.
- The facts a message expresses, including the offering, capabilities, pricing, and commercial terms: knowledge/company/ and knowledge/product/.
- Marketing strategy, channels, campaigns, audience targeting, and positioning strategy: the Marketing namespace, knowledge/marketing/.
- Voice, tone, vocabulary, and naming: their documents under knowledge/brand/.
- Company identity, mission, and principles: the documents in knowledge/company/.
- Legal constraints on claims and disclosures: the legal documents in their owning folders.
- Produced content, such as a specific headline, page, or advertisement: the Output level of the knowledge hierarchy and the asset systems.
- Content systems, rendering, and tooling: the codebase, not the knowledge repository.

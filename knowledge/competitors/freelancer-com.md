# Purpose

# Overview

Freelancer.com is a large, global freelance marketplace organized around competitive bidding. Businesses post projects, and freelancers compete for them by placing bids, alongside a contest format in which multiple entrants submit work and the buyer selects a winner.

# Marketplace Model

Freelancer.com operates as an open bidding marketplace. Businesses post a project, and freelancers submit bids to win it, where placing bids draws on a limited free allowance supplemented by paid bid packs or memberships. It also runs contests, where entrants submit competing work for a posted prize. Payment can be released against milestones and held until work is accepted, and the platform charges fees on projects. The specific current fees and terms are external and change over time.

# Target Customers

Freelancer.com serves a broad, global, and often price-sensitive demand side, and a very large international supply side of freelancers across many disciplines.

# Strengths

Freelancer.com's durable strengths include its large scale and global reach, a very large pool of available freelancers, the contest format for visual and creative work, and access to talent across a wide range of budgets.

# Tradeoffs

Freelancer.com's model carries inherent tradeoffs. Freelancers draw on a limited allowance and paid bids to compete for posted projects, and the large, bid-driven pool tends to compete strongly on price.

# Where It Excels

Freelancer.com tends to serve best for budget-conscious projects, for contests where a buyer wants many creative options, and where a buyer wants a large volume of bids to choose from.

# Relationship to Open Lance

Freelancer.com and Open Lance are both two-sided freelance marketplaces serving businesses and freelancers, so they overlap in audience. Their models differ, notably in how freelancers access and compete for work. Open Lance's own model is owned by knowledge/company/company.md and knowledge/product/, and how Open Lance positions itself relative to Freelancer.com is owned by the Marketing namespace. This document states the overlap and the difference as fact and argues no advantage.

# Purpose

This document owns a neutral, durable, factual description of Upwork. Its purpose is that every agent and contributor works from the same accurate understanding of Upwork, free of opinion or persuasion.

# Overview

Upwork is one of the largest and most established general freelance marketplaces, operating globally across a broad range of professional categories. It connects businesses that need work done with independent freelancers and agencies that provide it.

# Marketplace Model

Upwork operates as an open, general marketplace. Businesses post jobs or search for talent, and freelancers and agencies pursue work by submitting proposals, where submitting a proposal draws on a paid allowance of bidding credits. It supports both hourly engagements, with time tracking, and fixed-price engagements with milestone-based payment, and it holds committed funds until work is accepted. The platform charges service fees, historically applied on both the freelancer and the client side, and it extends to enterprise and managed offerings alongside a catalog of pre-packaged projects. The specific current fees and terms are external and change over time.

# Target Customers

Upwork serves a wide demand side, from individuals and small businesses to large enterprises, and a broad, global supply side of freelancers and agencies spanning many disciplines.

# Strengths

Upwork's durable strengths include its scale and the liquidity that comes with it, the breadth of its categories, strong brand recognition, mature payment protection and time tracking, and offerings that reach enterprise buyers.

# Tradeoffs

Upwork's model carries inherent tradeoffs. Freelancers draw on a paid allowance to submit proposals and compete within a large applicant pool, service fees apply to participation, and the same breadth that provides liquidity means buyers select from among many applicants.

# Where It Excels

Upwork tends to serve best where buyers want a wide selection across many categories, where hiring is large-scale or reaches into the enterprise, and where ongoing hourly engagements with tracked time suit the work.

# Relationship to Open Lance

Upwork and Open Lance are both general, two-sided freelance marketplaces serving businesses and freelancers across many categories, so they overlap in audience. Their models differ, notably in how freelancers access work and in fee structure. Open Lance's own marketplace model is owned by knowledge/company/company.md and knowledge/product/, and how Open Lance positions itself relative to Upwork is owned by the Marketing namespace. This document states the overlap and the difference as fact and argues no advantage.

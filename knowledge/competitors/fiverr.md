# Purpose

This document owns a neutral, durable, factual description of Fiverr. Its purpose is that every agent and contributor works from the same accurate understanding of Fiverr, free of opinion or persuasion.

# Overview

Fiverr is a large freelance marketplace organized around productized services. Rather than posting a job and reviewing applicants, buyers browse fixed-scope service listings and purchase them directly.

# Marketplace Model

Fiverr operates as a catalog of productized service listings. Sellers publish defined offerings at set prices, often in tiered packages, and buyers browse, compare, and purchase them directly. Discovery, search, and seller ratings drive matching, sellers progress through recognition levels based on their track record, and the platform holds payment until the buyer accepts the delivered work. The platform charges fees on transactions. The specific current fees and terms are external and change over time.

# Target Customers

Fiverr serves buyers who want a clearly defined deliverable quickly, and sellers who can package their skills into repeatable, fixed-scope offerings. It spans many creative and digital disciplines.

# Strengths

Fiverr's durable strengths include the simplicity and speed of buying a defined service, a very large catalog of ready-made offerings, strong discovery and search, and clear packaging that lets buyers know what they are getting before they buy.

# Tradeoffs

Fiverr's model carries inherent tradeoffs. Packaging work into fixed-scope listings suits well-defined tasks more than complex or evolving engagements, and a large catalog of comparable offerings tends to compete on visibility and price.

# Where It Excels

Fiverr tends to serve best where the work is well defined and repeatable, where a buyer wants speed and a known scope, and where a seller can productize a skill into a standard offering.

# Relationship to Open Lance

Fiverr and Open Lance overlap where work is offered as a productized, fixed-scope service. Whether and how Open Lance offers productized services is owned by knowledge/product/features.md; this document describes Fiverr's model. Their models differ in how work is discovered and scoped. Open Lance's own model is owned by knowledge/company/company.md and knowledge/product/, and how Open Lance positions itself relative to Fiverr is owned by the Marketing namespace. This document states the overlap and the difference as fact and argues no advantage.

# Open Lance Principles

This document defines how Open Lance makes decisions. It provides the enduring decision principles that guide every human and AI contributor whenever more than one reasonable choice exists. It is the universal decision framework for the company.

This document does not define who Open Lance is, where it is going, or what it does each day. It also does not define policies, processes, pricing, metrics, campaigns, or implementation. It defines only how decisions are made. Where this document and a higher-authority document appear to differ, the higher-authority document governs.

# Relationship to Other Documents

Open Lance separates four foundational questions across four documents, each with a single responsibility.

- knowledge/company/company.md defines who Open Lance is.
- knowledge/company/vision.md defines where Open Lance is going.
- knowledge/company/mission.md defines what Open Lance does every day.
- knowledge/company/principles.md, this document, defines how decisions are made.

These boundaries are strict. This document draws its principles from the first three and does not restate their content. When a principle depends on a value, destination, or daily commitment defined elsewhere, it references the owning document rather than repeating it.

# Core Principles

The principles below are derived from the values and commitments already stated in knowledge/company/company.md, knowledge/company/vision.md, and knowledge/company/mission.md. They are not new company values. Those documents state what Open Lance believes and will not compromise on; this document states how those commitments are applied when choosing between options. Where knowledge/company/vision.md already states a decision precedence or a trade-off method, this document references it rather than restating it.

Each principle is described by its purpose, meaning, effect on decisions, common mistakes, and the point at which a contributor must escalate.

## Protect trust above short-term gain

- Purpose. Trust is the asset the marketplace depends on and the hardest to rebuild. This principle keeps it from being spent for speed.
- Meaning. When a choice would improve a near-term result but weaken trust, integrity, or honesty, it is not taken.
- How it affects decisions. Between two options, prefer the one that preserves trust even when it is slower or smaller.
- Common mistakes. Treating a one-time trust cost as acceptable because the gain is large; assuming trust can be repaired later.
- When to escalate. When a goal cannot be met without reducing trust, halt and escalate.
- Cross-references. knowledge/company/vision.md (trust as the core asset and its precedence); knowledge/company/mission.md (trust).

## Serve both sides fairly

- Purpose. Open Lance exists for businesses and freelancers together. This principle prevents decisions that quietly favor one at the other's expense.
- Meaning. A decision may benefit one side, but not at the expense of the other. Fairness to both is required.
- How it affects decisions. Check each option for harm to the side it does not directly serve, and prefer options that keep both safe.
- Common mistakes. Optimizing only for the side a contributor is assigned to; reading any benefit to one side as harm to the other.
- When to escalate. When both sides cannot be served and the conflict is material, resolve it by the trade-off method below, and escalate if that does not resolve it.
- Cross-references. knowledge/company/company.md (fairness); knowledge/company/mission.md (both sides); knowledge/company/vision.md (protect both sides).

## Choose transparency and honesty

- Purpose. Clear, honest decisions are explainable and resist abuse.
- Meaning. Prefer the option that is honest and can be explained plainly, and never mislead, overstate, omit, or invent.
- How it affects decisions. When an option depends on concealment to work, that is a signal to reject it.
- Common mistakes. Presenting a favorable claim without support; withholding a material fact because disclosing it is inconvenient.
- When to escalate. When a truthful account would undermine the decision, halt and escalate rather than shade the truth.
- Cross-references. knowledge/company/company.md (transparency); knowledge/company/mission.md (transparency).

## Protect security and safety

- Purpose. Participants entrust Open Lance with their work, funds, and data.
- Meaning. Decisions protect participant security and marketplace safety by default and never trade them for convenience.
- How it affects decisions. Prefer the option that reduces risk to participants, even at some cost to speed or effort.
- Common mistakes. Deferring a safeguard to a later step; treating a low-probability harm as no harm.
- When to escalate. When a decision would weaken security or safety, halt and escalate.
- Cross-references. knowledge/company/mission.md (security); knowledge/company/vision.md (protect users and marketplace safety).

## Respect participant privacy

- Purpose. Participants are not a public catalog, and their standing and information deserve protection.
- Meaning. Do not expose participants or their information beyond what a decision genuinely requires.
- How it affects decisions. Prefer the option that reveals the least about participants while still achieving the goal.
- Common mistakes. Collecting or surfacing more than needed because it is available.
- When to escalate. When a decision would expose participants beyond what is necessary, halt and escalate.
- Cross-references. knowledge/company/company.md (privacy); knowledge/company/vision.md (participant privacy and dignity).

## Favor durability and long-term success

- Purpose. Open Lance aims to help both sides build long-term success, which requires durable choices.
- Meaning. Prefer decisions that will still look correct as time passes over ones that only look good now.
- How it affects decisions. Weigh the durable version of an option, not only its immediate effect.
- Common mistakes. Choosing a fragile shortcut for an immediate result; discounting long-term cost because it is deferred.
- When to escalate. When a durable option and an urgent one genuinely conflict and cannot be reconciled, escalate.
- Cross-references. knowledge/company/mission.md (long-term success); knowledge/company/vision.md (long-term sustainability).

# Decision Hierarchy

Open Lance already defines an authoritative precedence order, and this document does not redefine or reorder it. When decisions or documents conflict, the Knowledge Hierarchy in knowledge/README.md governs, and these principles are one level within it, at the Principles authority.

Two constraints bound every decision.

- Applicable law and binding legal obligations are an absolute constraint. No decision, and no principle in this document, may justify violating the law. This is consistent with knowledge/company/mission.md, which states that the mission does not override legal obligations, and with the treatment of Legal in knowledge/README.md.
- Higher-authority documents govern lower ones. A decision must be consistent with knowledge/company/company.md, knowledge/company/vision.md, and knowledge/company/mission.md before these principles are applied.

When two of these principles conflict with one another, they are ordered by the Principle Precedence defined in knowledge/company/vision.md. This document does not restate that order.

# Decision Framework

Contributors can apply the following method to any decision, regardless of product, pricing, technology, or organizational size.

1. Confirm legality. If a choice may violate a legal obligation, stop and escalate. Law is never traded against.
2. Check higher authority. Confirm the choice is consistent with knowledge/company/company.md, knowledge/company/vision.md, and knowledge/company/mission.md.
3. Apply the principles. Evaluate the remaining options against the Core Principles above.
4. Resolve conflicts by precedence. If principles pull in different directions, order them using the Principle Precedence in knowledge/company/vision.md.
5. If still unresolved, use the trade-off method described in Handling Trade-offs below.
6. If a confident decision still cannot be reached, halt and escalate.

The decision-quality guidance in knowledge/company/vision.md, namely favoring simplicity, deciding from evidence, and preferring reversible decisions when uncertain, applies to every decision and is referenced here rather than restated. This framework complements, and does not replace, the near-term checklist in knowledge/company/mission.md or the long-term checks in knowledge/company/vision.md.

# Handling Trade-offs

Principles can appear to conflict. When they do, the goal is to honor the higher-priority principle while doing the least harm, not to invent a new rule for the situation.

The reasoning method is already defined and is not restated here. Order the conflicting principles using the Principle Precedence in knowledge/company/vision.md, then resolve the specific choice using the Trade-off Protocol in knowledge/company/vision.md. A trade-off is never resolved by creating a new principle, a new policy, or an exception. If the method does not produce a confident decision, halt and escalate.

# Escalation

Contributors and AI agents must halt and escalate, rather than proceed, when any of the following is true.

- A choice may violate a legal obligation.
- A choice would require contradicting a higher-authority document.
- Principles conflict and the precedence and trade-off method do not resolve the conflict with confidence.
- A decision is high-impact or hard to reverse and its correctness is unclear.
- Knowledge required to decide is missing and cannot be obtained.

Escalation follows the process defined in knowledge/CONTRIBUTING.md. This document does not define who receives an escalation or how it is routed.

# What Principles Do NOT Do

- Principles do not replace laws. Legal obligations bind regardless of any principle.
- Principles do not replace policies. Standing decisions live in policy documents, not here.
- Principles do not replace processes. How work is carried out lives in process documents, not here.
- Principles do not determine pricing. Pricing and commercial terms live in their own documents.
- Principles do not approve exceptions. No principle authorizes a deviation from a higher-authority document.
- Principles do not set strategy or daily tasks. Those belong to knowledge/company/vision.md and knowledge/company/mission.md.

# AI Decision Checklist

An AI agent confirms the following before producing work. If any answer is no or unclear, the agent halts and escalates per knowledge/CONTRIBUTING.md.

- The work does not risk violating a legal obligation.
- The work is consistent with knowledge/company/company.md, knowledge/company/vision.md, and knowledge/company/mission.md.
- The work protects trust and does not spend it for a short-term gain.
- The work serves businesses and freelancers fairly, without advantaging one side at the other's expense.
- The work is transparent and honest, with no misleading, overstated, or invented content.
- The work protects participant security and privacy.
- The work favors durable outcomes and stays within this document's scope.
- Any conflict between principles was resolved by the precedence and trade-off guidance in knowledge/company/vision.md, or escalated.

This checklist applies the Core Principles above. It complements, and does not replace, the checklists in knowledge/company/mission.md and knowledge/company/vision.md.

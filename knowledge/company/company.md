# Company Overview

Open Lance is a two-sided freelance marketplace that connects businesses (clients) who post work with freelancers who find work, deliver it, and get paid. The brand tagline is "Built by freelancers, for freelancers."

Every engagement is protected by escrow: the client funds work into escrow before it begins, and funds release to the freelancer only when the client approves the work. The platform is designed to be fair to both sides, with secure payments and neutral dispute resolution.

Open Lance is a product and brand. The operating company (and payment account holder) is Brilliant Systems LLC, a company operating under the jurisdiction of the State of Colorado, USA. The official copyright and ownership line is: "Open Lance © {current year} Brilliant Systems LLC. All rights reserved." Always render `{current year}` as the actual current year at the time of use; never hardcode a fixed year.

Communication channels, websites, social media accounts, and contact information are maintained separately in knowledge/company/communication.md.

Brand-identity note (important for search, content, and SEO work): the name "Open Lance" collides with several unrelated entities, including openlance.ai, the Lance data format, and OPENLANE. Always tie the brand to Open Lance and Brilliant Systems LLC so it is not confused with them.

# Mission

Open Lance empowers businesses and freelancers through a transparent, secure, and fair marketplace with escrow-backed payments and tools that help both sides build long-term success.

# Vision

To become the world's most trusted freelance marketplace where businesses and freelancers collaborate without unfair fees, hidden barriers, or unnecessary complexity.

# Why Open Lance Exists

Open Lance was built by freelancers who were tired of losing a chunk of every invoice and waiting days to get paid, often unsure whether payment would arrive at all. The company exists to remove those problems structurally:

- Get paid on approval: when both parties agree to instant release, approved milestones pay out immediately with no clearance wait.
- Never gamble on payment: work is funded into escrow before it starts, so freelancers can see the money is there before they begin.
- Fair terms by default: fairness to both sides is built into how the marketplace works, rather than something a freelancer has to negotiate or hope for.

# Problems in the Freelance Industry

The industry problems Open Lance is built to solve:

- High commissions. Traditional platforms take a significant cut of every freelancer invoice.
- Slow, risky payouts. Freelancers wait days for funds and chase invoices, with real risk that payment never comes.
- Payment uncertainty for both sides. Freelancers are unsure they will be paid; clients are unsure the work will be delivered.
- Pay-to-win bidding. On some platforms, bigger budgets or escalating bid costs beat reputation, disadvantaging strong-but-cheaper talent.
- Weak dispute handling. When work goes sideways, resolution is often opaque, slow, or one-sided.
- Trust and privacy gaps. Public talent directories expose freelancers, and reputation signals are easy to game.

# Our Solution

Open Lance addresses those problems with a single, coherent model:

- Escrow on every job. Clients fund milestones into protected escrow before work begins; funds release only on client approval.
- Instant release option. When both parties agree to instant release, approved milestones reach the freelancer's wallet immediately.
- Fair dispute resolution. Neutral, structured mediation reviews both sides; any partial settlement is held in escrow for a short security period, never paid out hastily.
- Fair bidding. Proposals compete on reputation and fit, not budget. There is no pay-to-win: a larger budget does not buy better placement.
- Privacy-first talent. Talent is not a public catalog; clients discover freelancers after signing in, and profiles are shared with a client only when the freelancer engages with them.
- Earned, verifiable reputation. Reviews are tied to completed contracts. Verification levels and the Open Lance Talent Level signal credibility without exposing freelancers in a public list. Freelancers can also import external track record to start ranked.

# Marketplace Model

Open Lance is a two-sided marketplace with four distinct ways to hire, each mapped to a real engagement type:

1. Drop-Ins. Productized, fixed-scope services. A freelancer lists a ready-made, fixed-scope offering; a client buys it with no back-and-forth brief. Escrow-protected like everything else.
2. Fixed-price project. The client posts a job, agrees milestones up front, and releases payment as each milestone is approved.
3. Hourly. Hire by the hour with a transparent work diary; the client reviews and approves logged hours.
4. Dedicated Hire. An ongoing monthly engagement, effectively a dedicated team member managed end-to-end on Open Lance.

Core marketplace flow (both sides):
`sign up -> complete/verify profile -> post job or list a Drop-In / submit proposal -> fund milestone into escrow -> deliver work -> client approves -> escrow releases (instant or after a short security period) -> reviews -> repeat hire`

Teams: freelancers are not limited to solo work. A freelancer can bring on bidders and teammates who bid and work on their behalf, allowing them to take on more work and grow beyond a single operator.

Pricing, subscriptions, fees, and commercial policies are maintained separately within the pricing knowledge documents.

Talent categories offered: Development & IT, Design & Creative, Writing & Translation, Sales & Marketing, Data & Analytics, and Mobile & Web.

# Success Metrics

Operational metrics are maintained separately inside knowledge/company/metrics.md and must never be hardcoded into this document.

Open Lance measures success by real, completed transactions (work that is funded into escrow, delivered, and released), not by signups or other vanity metrics. A small number of genuinely completed transactions matters more than a large volume of accounts.

Core funnel to track (counts belong in metrics.md, not here):
`signup -> profile/verify -> first proposal or Drop-In live -> first funded contract -> delivered and released -> repeat hire`

Leading indicators that best predict marketplace health:
- Time to first funded contract for a new client (shorter is better).
- Repeat hire rate: whether clients return without being prompted, an early signal of product-market fit.

Vanity metrics (page views, total signups) are explicitly deprioritized in favor of funded-and-released contracts.

# Important Rules AI Agents Must Remember

These rules are specific to representing Open Lance accurately in content, growth, outreach, support, and product work. They complement the Non-Negotiable Principles above.

1. Pricing lives in the pricing knowledge documents, not here. Treat pricing, subscriptions, fees, and commercial policies as operational and subject to change. Always use the canonical pricing source, present pricing as current rather than permanent, and never state pricing that conflicts with that source.
2. No fake marketplace activity, ever. No fake jobs, fake freelancers, or fake engagement. Being early is a strength; protect trust.
3. Value first in outreach. Do not spam or send identical mass messages. Personalize, lead with what the recipient gains, and give value before pitching.
4. Represent escrow and payouts correctly. Work is funded into escrow before it begins and released only on client approval. Instant release applies only when both parties agree; otherwise funds clear after a short security period.
5. Represent bidding correctly. Proposals compete on reputation and fit, not budget. There is no pay-to-win; a larger budget does not buy better placement. Do not state proposal or bid pricing here; defer to the pricing knowledge documents.
6. Respect talent privacy. There is no public freelancer directory. Clients discover talent after signing in; do not imply profiles are publicly browsable.
7. Do not over-discount to acquire signups. Attract users who want the product, not a coupon.
8. Never use the long em dash in external copy, UI text, or content. Use commas, colons, periods, or parentheses instead.

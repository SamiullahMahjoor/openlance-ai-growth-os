# Overview

PeoplePerHour is a freelance marketplace that combines posted projects with productized service offers. Businesses can post work for freelancers to propose to, and can also browse ready-made offers that freelancers publish at set prices.

# Marketplace Model

PeoplePerHour operates as a hybrid marketplace. On one path, businesses post a project and freelancers submit proposals, where submitting proposals draws on a limited allowance supplemented by paid credits. On the other path, freelancers publish productized offers at set prices that buyers purchase directly. It supports hourly and project work, and it holds committed funds until work is accepted. The platform charges fees on transactions. The specific current fees and terms are external and change over time.

# Target Customers

PeoplePerHour serves small and medium businesses seeking either custom work or ready-made offers, and a broad base of freelancers, with a long-standing presence among United Kingdom and European users alongside a global reach.

# Strengths

PeoplePerHour's durable strengths include offering both custom proposals and productized offers in one place, an established base of buyers and freelancers, and payment held until work is accepted.

# Tradeoffs

PeoplePerHour's model carries inherent tradeoffs. Submitting proposals draws on a limited allowance and paid credits, and combining custom and productized paths means buyers navigate two ways of engaging.

# Where It Excels

PeoplePerHour tends to serve best for small and medium business projects, for buyers who want the option of either a custom proposal or a ready-made offer, and where an established regional base is valuable.

# Relationship to Open Lance

PeoplePerHour and Open Lance overlap in serving businesses and freelancers. Whether and how Open Lance offers open engagements and productized offers is owned by knowledge/product/features.md; this document describes PeoplePerHour's model. Their models differ, notably in how freelancers access proposals. Open Lance's own model is owned by knowledge/company/company.md and knowledge/product/, and how Open Lance positions itself relative to PeoplePerHour is owned by the Marketing namespace. This document states the overlap and the difference as fact and argues no advantage.
